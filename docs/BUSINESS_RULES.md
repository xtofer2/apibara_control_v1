# Apibara Control - Business Rules V1

## 1. Operational shift

Each location operates through an operational shift (`work_shift`).

A location can have only one open shift at a time.

A normal shift lifecycle is:

```text
OPEN
-> CLOSED
```

A shift must have one opening before normal operational movements are accepted.

Once closed, normal employee operations must not modify it.

Corrections after closure require a controlled manager/admin adjustment or administrative workflow that preserves audit history.

## 2. Opening

An opening records:

- location
- operational shift
- employee
- server-generated date/time
- physical count of active products
- initial physical cash

An opening cannot be duplicated for the same shift.

Employees must not manually set the operation timestamp.

Opening data should not be silently overwritten after confirmation.

## 3. Inventory movements

V1 supports these internal movement types:

- ENTRY
- WASTE
- ADJUSTMENT_POSITIVE
- ADJUSTMENT_NEGATIVE

Transfers between locations are modeled separately.

An inventory movement may contain multiple products.

Quantities must be non-negative values; movement type determines whether their effect is positive or negative.

## 4. Waste

Waste decreases available inventory.

Waste requires a reason.

Recommended reasons:

- DAMAGED
- DROPPED
- PREPARATION
- EXPIRED
- OTHER

If reason is OTHER, notes are mandatory.

Waste records are auditable.

## 5. Inventory adjustments

Adjustments exist to correct discrepancies or errors.

Only manager/admin roles can create inventory adjustments in V1.

Adjustment reason/notes are mandatory.

Do not use waste to hide a counting correction.

Do not use adjustments to hide a transfer discrepancy.

## 6. Transfers between locations

A transfer has an origin and destination location.

Origin and destination must be different.

Normal lifecycle:

```text
PENDING
-> SENT
-> RECEIVED
```

Alternative lifecycle:

```text
PENDING
-> SENT
-> RECEIVED_WITH_DIFFERENCES
```

A transfer may also become CANCELLED according to authorization rules.

### Sending

When the origin confirms sending:

- `sent_by` is recorded
- `sent_at` is generated by the system
- status becomes SENT
- quantities are considered to have left the origin

### Receiving

The destination employee must explicitly confirm actual quantities received.

Receiving records:

- `received_by`
- `received_at`
- actual received quantity for each item

Destination inventory is affected only when receipt is confirmed.

A transfer cannot be received twice.

### Transfer differences

If any received quantity differs from sent quantity:

- status becomes RECEIVED_WITH_DIFFERENCES
- reception notes are mandatory
- only received quantities affect destination inventory
- discrepancy stays tied to the transfer

Example:

```text
Sent:      20 classic
Received:  19 classic
Difference: -1
```

The missing unit must not automatically become destination waste.

### Transfers at shift closing

The origin shift may close even if a sent transfer is still pending reception.

The transfer remains in transit until the destination confirms it.

## 7. Closing

Closing records:

- employee
- server-generated timestamp
- physical final count for each active product
- closing payment amounts by payment method

A shift has at most one closing.

After closing, shift status becomes CLOSED.

## 8. Payment methods

V1 supports:

- CASH
- YAPE

Opening records only initial physical cash.

Closing records payment amounts separately.

Example:

```text
Cash: S/ 200
Yape: S/ 150
Calculated closing total: S/ 350
```

The total is calculated from closing payment records and should not be redundantly stored.

## 9. Calculated sales

Individual sales are not recorded during the day.

For each product, calculated sales after closing are:

```text
opening
+ entries
+ confirmed transfer receipts
+ positive adjustments
- sent transfers
- waste
- negative adjustments
- closing
```

This is a reconciliation result, not a persisted sale transaction.

Do not create a `sales` table in V1 for these calculated values.

## 10. Real-time stock limitation

Because individual sales are not captured, the system does not know exact physical real-time stock during the shift.

It can know accounting movements but not how much has already been sold before closing.

Therefore V1 must not rely on an exact real-time stock check to validate a transfer.

Employees are responsible for physically verifying product availability before sending.

## 11. Attendance

Attendance is independent from operational actions.

An employee may work without creating an opening, closing, transfer, or waste record.

Attendance records:

- employee
- location
- work date
- check-in timestamp
- optional check-out timestamp

The manager uses attendance to determine who worked each day.

## 12. Auditability

Sensitive operations must preserve:

- who acted
- what action occurred
- entity involved
- before/after data when relevant
- timestamp

Confirmed operational records must not be hard-deleted in normal workflows.

Relevant audited actions include:

- CREATE
- UPDATE
- CONFIRM
- CANCEL
- ADJUST

## 13. Product units

Product catalog determines unit type.

UNIT products must use whole-number quantities.

LITER products can use decimal quantities.

Initial products:

- Empanada Clasica / UNIT
- Empanada Pizza / UNIT
- Empanada Full Queso / UNIT
- Api Morado / LITER
- Api Blanco / LITER
- Emoliente / LITER

## 14. Initial roles and permissions

### Employee

Allowed:

- check in/out
- open shift
- close shift
- register entry
- register waste
- send transfer
- receive transfer

Not allowed:

- administrative inventory adjustments
- user administration
- unrestricted audit access

### Manager

Allowed additionally:

- view all locations and shifts
- view reports
- view calculated sales
- view attendance
- view audit logs
- create inventory adjustments
- review transfer discrepancies

### Admin

Allowed additionally:

- manage users
- manage roles
- manage locations
- manage configuration

## 15. Validation principles

Frontend validation improves user experience.

Server/domain validation enforces business rules.

PostgreSQL constraints protect data integrity.

RLS protects access boundaries.

Critical multi-step operations must use transactions.
